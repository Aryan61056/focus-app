Visual directions for the Quiet style

Three ways to give the same product a face. They share the behavior, the copy and the calm rules, and differ on one question: how much identity should the app carry on top of the operating system?

A: NativeB: LichenC: Graphite
IdeaBorrow the system accent and materials. The ring and the words are the identity.One quiet green, only for on task and the primary action. Rounder, warmer.No accent. Emphasis by inversion: a dark slab on light, a light slab on dark.
ColorSystem accent (blue by default, follows the user's choice). Neutral grays.Hue 156, tinted neutrals. Deep green fill on light, light green on dark.Pure neutrals. Ink is the only "color".
RingThin circle, 1.5 stroke.Bold circle with round caps; doubles as session progress in the pill.Thin squircle, 1.3 stroke. Most distinct silhouette.
PillStandard 30 px capsule.38 px capsule, larger ring, rounded numerals.26 px plate, mono numerals, fades to a faint plate at rest.
NudgeGlass card, three buttons in a row, accent primary.Glass card, one full-width green button, two quiet text buttons below.Inverted slab, three rows like a menu, default marked with Return.
StartSpotlight-style bar, selected row, chips.A sheet led by one large Continue button.Docked keyboard-first bar, inverted selection, text toggles.
SummaryGrouped list, thin timeline.A sentence, then a block timeline.Typeset table, tick timeline.
StrengthLooks like it shipped with the OS. Respects the user's accent. Least risk.Ownable and warm without shouting. One color means one thing.The most discreet and professional. Easiest to carry through Windows themes and high contrast.
RiskHard to tell from system UI. Weak recognition.Ignores the user's system accent. Green can read as "success", so it must stay off error and warning.The inverted slab is the loudest nudge of the three, which cuts against "loudness follows state". Coldest.

Recommendation

B, with the card layout softened toward A's quiet buttons if the first tests say it is too prominent, and a Settings switch to follow the system accent instead of green. B is calm and carries identity with one color, and its single filled button matches the "one primary action" rule on every surface. A is the safe fallback. C is the best fit for a "no trace" confidential mode, but as the default its inverted card is too loud for a gentle nudge.

This is a recommendation from mockups only. Real Liquid Glass and Fluent rendering, ring legibility in a real menu bar, and how the nudge feels in use are untested until a native prototype exists.
