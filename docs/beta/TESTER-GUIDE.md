# Testing Focus

Thank you for trying Focus before anyone else. This page is everything you need. It takes two minutes to read.

## What Focus is

You tell Focus what you are working on. While a session runs, Apple's on-device model compares the window in front with your task, and when you drift for a while Focus nudges you back. Everything runs on your Mac.

You need a Mac with Apple silicon and Apple Intelligence turned on (System Settings, Apple Intelligence and Siri).

## Install

1. Open the `.dmg` and drag Focus onto Applications.
2. Open Focus from Applications. If macOS says it cannot check the app, choose Done, then open System Settings, Privacy & Security, and choose Open Anyway next to Focus. You only do this once per build.
3. Focus asks for Accessibility the first time it needs to see which window you are in. Screen Recording is asked for only if you turn on screen reading.

A newer beta installs the same way, over the old one. Your sessions and settings stay.

## What we ask

Use Focus for real work, on at least three days, for about a week. Settings > Beta lists seven things worth trying; they tick themselves as you go, and the ring on your profile (bottom left) shows how far you are.

Once a week, between sessions, Today asks how Focus is working for you. One click on five dots. Skip is fine.

## Sending feedback

Click your name at the bottom left of the Focus window, then **Send feedback**. Or Help > Send Feedback (Option-Command-F).

1. Pick what it is: something broke, a wrong call, an idea, or anything else.
2. Write what happened in your own words. For a wrong call, describe the *kind* of window ("a PDF of my lecture notes") rather than what was in it.
3. Leave the checkboxes on unless you would rather not share that part. **See the whole file** shows exactly what will be saved.
4. Save the report and send the file to whoever gave you the build (or press Email report, if your build has it: your mail app opens a draft with the file attached, and you press Send).

If Focus closed unexpectedly, Today offers to write a report the next time you open it. That one helps most.

## What a report holds, and what it never holds

It holds what you write, the build, your Mac's model and macOS version, whether permissions are on, your settings switches, counts (sessions, minutes, nudges) and a log of when things happened, plus where in Focus's code a crash happened if there was one.

It never holds your task or project names, window titles, web addresses, anything read from your screen, or pictures. Focus does not send anything by itself: the report is a file you can open and read before you send it.

The beta log stays on your Mac. Settings > Beta lets you turn it off or clear it.

## When the beta ends

Each build has an end date. After it, Focus keeps working and says a newer build is waiting. Ask for it.

## Things that are not finished

Focus can be wrong. When it is not sure, it does nothing. A wrong call is exactly the kind of report we want.
