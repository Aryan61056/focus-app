# Ways to get you back on track (2026-10-16)

Owner ask: "find more better ways to nudge", get Back to task to the correct area, and more ideas like removing the color from the screen.
All of it stays inside the rules in CLAUDE.md: on-device, nothing pushed names the distraction, a false nudge costs more than a miss, and every new cue is a switch the user can turn off.

## What already existed
Heads-up banner, reminder banner, notch card, menu bar tint, Dock bounce, trackpad taps, voice, the gray screen, check-in, pause screen, hiding the app, switching or closing the tab, the cover when the same site opens again, scheduled check-ins and breaks. See `Cues.swift`, `Escalation.swift`.

## Built in this pass
| Idea | What it does | Switch |
|---|---|---|
| **Back to task goes to the place** | Remembers the few windows, tabs and documents you worked in (names only, memory only, dropped with the session) and how long you stayed in each. Goes to the one you settled in (45 s or more), else the one you spent the most time in, so a glance at a reference never becomes "the task". Raises the exact window (Accessibility: same file, then same title), or the exact tab in any browser (AppleScript: by address, then by title), even when you have already left for another app. If it cannot find the place it tries the next, and says it only reached the app. | Settings > Switch back to your tab (tabs only) |
| **Pick from the menu** | The menu bar shows where Back to task lands (icon and short name) and up to two other places to go instead. Tooltips and VoiceOver say the same on the banner, cards, notch and pause screen. | none |
| **Landing ring** | A soft ring on the window that came forward, for about a second and a half. Fainter when only the app came forward. A reward, not a nudge; no motion with Reduce Motion. | Settings > Ring the window you land in (on) |
| **Close in the edges** | The edges of every display darken and close toward the middle as you stay away, the middle stays readable, it opens again when you return. Same ramp as the gray; works with it or alone. | Settings > Close in the edges (off) |

## Built later (2026-10-17): more situations where Back to task works
| Situation | What it does | Switch |
|---|---|---|
| **The place was closed** | A document, folder (a Finder window, an editor's project) or web page you were working in is opened again; an app that quit is started with it. Only things you had open and Focus read as on task, only on a trip you asked for (never the ladder's own), never programs or scripts, only http(s) pages, and a page only when the browser said for certain that no such tab exists. See `Reopen.swift`. | Settings > Open it again if it was closed (on) |
| **Tabs in any app** | The tab bar of Terminal, Finder, Firefox, Preview, TextEdit, Xcode and others is pressed by title when no script can reach the tab. | with Switch back to your tab |
| **Electron apps** | VS Code, Slack, Notion, Discord and Obsidian show their windows to Accessibility only when asked; Focus asks when it finds none. The trip after that finds them. | none |
| **The whole task** | Up to two other windows you really worked in (45 s or more) come forward first, oldest first, then the main place on top, so the distraction ends up behind all of them. | Settings > Bring your other windows too (on) |
| **Back to your spot** | While you work, the scroll position and cursor offset of the window are noted every 3 s (numbers only, memory only, never while you are somewhere off task). After the trip they are put back, a few tries a second apart. The cursor is not restored if the text changed too much for the offset to mean the same place. | Settings > Back to your spot (on) |
| **A link or the Dock** | `focusapp://back` (Shortcuts "Open URLs", Raycast, Alfred, a Stream Deck button) and the Dock icon's menu do Back to task during a session. Only that one action is understood; a burst of links counts once (a page cannot loop it). The scheme is a working name and changes with the product name. The URL type is in `tools/build_app.sh`. | Settings > Let a link or the Dock do it (on) |
| **Put the drifted app away** | After a trip you asked for, the app you drifted into is hidden (Command-H; nothing closed). Never the task's app, a place you worked in, Focus, Finder, system apps or the never-judged list, never in practice, never on the ladder's own return. | Settings > Put it away when I go back (off) |
| **Mark the way back** (beacon) | While Focus is nudging you (a real nudge, not the first doubt), a faint ring outlines the window Back to task would take you to, even with something on top of it. It follows the window if it moves and goes the moment you are back. Click-through, asks for nothing; breathes slowly unless Reduce Motion is on. In a browser whose front tab is another one, the app's only window is outlined. | Settings > Mark the way back (off) |
| **Gray only the window you drifted into** | The gray covers just the window you are off task in (its frame, followed at 10 Hz), so the task window beside it stays in color and looks like the way back. If Focus cannot tell which window it is (Focus itself is in front, the frame is unknown, or it is a place you worked in) nothing is grayed. The edges, if on, still close in on every display. Settings Preview always grays the whole screen. A panel over a window also covers any window that overlaps it. | Settings > Gray only the window you drifted into (off) |
| **What brings you back** (Insights) | Each time you drift and come back, Focus saves which of its cues had appeared last (banner, nudge card, gray, closing edges, check-in, pause screen, app hidden, or none) and how many seconds after it appeared you were back. Kinds and seconds only, no windows or text. Insights shows the typical time per cue over 30 days, fastest first, when at least two cues have three or more returns. It reports what was on screen, not what caused the return, and says so; nothing is chosen or skipped because of it. | none (part of Insights; practice sessions are left out) |
| **A ring around the pointer** | While you are off task a ring appears around the mouse and grows with the same ramp as the gray and the edges, from 20 to 110 points, click-through. Looks at the pointer 30 times a second while showing and twice a second while waiting. Its cost while showing has not been measured against the 2 percent budget (PLAN 9). | Settings > A ring around the pointer (off), with Preview |
| **Ease the sound down** | While Focus is nudging you (a real nudge) on a video site (YouTube, Netflix, Twitch, TikTok, Hulu, Disney+, Prime Video, Vimeo), the Mac's volume eases down over a few seconds to about 35 percent of where you had it, never to silence, and comes back when you return, pause, the session ends, or Focus quits. If you change the volume yourself it lets go for the rest of that nudge and never puts "your old volume" over yours. A volume already under 8 percent, an output that cannot be set, practice, and calls (the session is paused) are left alone. The lowered volume is remembered at every step so a crash or force quit is put right at the next launch, but only if the volume is still where Focus left it. Not run against real audio here (setting your volume was not something to do unasked); the CoreAudio calls are compile-checked. | Settings > Ease the sound down (off) |
| **Fog** | A progressive blur of the screen (up to about 14 points: it can be made out but not read) on the same private backdrop as the gray, with the same ramp. It goes where the gray goes: every display, or only the window you drifted into. Without the window server's filter classes it falls back to a pale veil. The Settings Preview fogs the whole screen. It is one of the cues counted in "What brings you back". Harsh by design. | Settings > Fog the screen (off), with Preview |
| **When slips start** (Insights) | Six small bars: how many slips began 0 to 10, 10 to 20 ... 50+ minutes into a session, over 30 days. Minute only, never what you drifted into. Shown after at least 4 sessions with 8 slips; a peak is named only when one step holds at least 40 percent and 4 slips. A flat spread is left as a spread. No prompt, nothing pushed. | none |
| **Spaces in one window (Arc, Dia)** | Owner, 2026-10-17: "different spaces in the same window ... its going to get complicated". A place there is a tab in a space, so the window cannot say where you were. Their scripting dictionaries (`sdef`) were read: Arc has `spaces` of a window, `tabs` of a space, `focus` on a space and `select` on a tab; Dia has `profiles` (its spaces), `tabs` of a profile and a `focus` command for a tab or a profile. Now: the space (Arc: its id; Dia: the profile name) is part of a place's identity, so the same page in two spaces is two places and the one you worked in wins; Back to task looks through every window's spaces (yours first), brings that space forward, then the tab; the menu and tooltips name it ("Arc · Work · Lecture notes"); Arc's private windows return nothing; Dia is never asked for an address (its dictionary has no private-window flag), only the profile name, and its tab is found by title; Arc/Dia now take part in the ladder's tab switch and close too. Scroll and cursor are not noted or restored in any browser (each tab keeps its own scroll), and the beacon outlines nothing when the way back is a tab inside the window you are in (outlining your own window would mislead). If Automation is refused, the app's tab bar is tried. | with Switch back to your tab |
| **A key from any app** | Control-Option-B (or R, or Control-Command-B, or off) does Back to task during a session. | Settings > Shortcut |

Checked on this Mac on 2026-10-17 against a throwaway helper app with Cocoa controls (a text view in a scroll view, a tab view): raising a window by title, the window's frame, pressing a tab by title (the tab really switched), reading the cursor offset, text length and scroll position, and putting scroll and cursor back. In 12 rounds in a row the restore worked on the first try. With another app in front the restore correctly refuses (it never writes into a window that is not the one asked for); after Focus's own raise (`WindowRaiser.raise` plus `bringForward`, the same calls `Place.go` makes) it worked and the cursor landed where it was. A plain `NSRunningApplication.activate()` did not bring the helper forward, which is why the app uses the Accessibility raise as well. One earlier run had a transient failure restoring a second time; it did not come back in the 12 rounds, and the controller retries four times a second apart anyway. The Arc and Dia AppleScripts compile against the installed dictionaries (`osacompile`) and, since 2026-10-17, run through `tools/check_space_scripts.sh`: 30 cases (the space or profile you worked in first, any other as a fallback, the tab and space brought forward, the window to the front, `none` and `changed` answers, quotes and backslashes in titles, a private window reading nothing, closing a tab only while it is still the page) against stand-in apps that follow the two dictionaries, with no permission prompt. That tests the scripts' logic through a real Cocoa scripting layer, not the real browsers; the real browsers; those were checked on the owner's Mac on 2026-10-17 through the Terminal panel (which has Automation), read-only and printing counts only: **Arc**: the active space and front tab's address read fine (6 spaces); walking every window, space and tab took 0.46 s for 25 tabs, so the app's 3 second cap is about 150 tabs away; `focus` on a space and `select` on a tab, sent through loop references to the space and tab already in front, were accepted. **Dia**: the active profile read fine (3 profiles); a walk of 9 tabs took 0.29 s; `focus` on a profile and a tab through loop references was accepted. **Found and fixed:** real Arc refuses a stored reference (`set t to active tab of front window` fails with -1700), so the old close script never matched and always answered "changed" (it failed safe); it now finds the tab by id through loop references (its logic ran on real Arc with the `close` swapped for a harmless return: same page matched, another page left alone). `set index of w to 1` fails on real Arc (-10000), so after an Arc or Dia script picks a tab the app also raises that window with Accessibility. Dia accepts stored references. **The real thing, verified the same day with `tools/check_space_browsers.sh --switch`:** the app's own script switched Arc to a tab in a different space (by address) and back, and Dia to a tab in a different profile (by title, which is how Dia is steered because its addresses are never read) and back; each landed where it should, both ways. (The first attempt returned Dia by address, which a Dia tab without a web address cannot do, and left Dia on the other profile until it was put back by hand: the check now uses the app's real Dia path and puts your space or profile back itself if the way back fails.) The app's own `Place.go` (what Back to task calls) was then run against both real browsers from a throwaway harness (not kept): Arc and Dia each went `exact` into a tab in another space or profile and back, and answered `app only` (never `exact`, nothing moved) for a page that is not open; the real `currentTab` output parsed to an address and a space for Arc and to a profile with no address for Dia. **Dia's close ran for real** on a throwaway tab made with `make new tab` (a localhost address nothing listens on, so no traffic): the app's close script closed it and the tab count came back. **Arc's close ran for real too** (later the same day, after the owner said closing a real tab is fine): the app's own close script, run on the tab in front, answered `ok`, that space went from 4 tabs to 3, and the tab in front changed. **Two windows on the same space or profile**, checked with a second window made and closed by a throwaway harness in each browser: `Place.go` to a tab answered `exact` in both, but neither browser brought the other window forward by script. The tab seems to come up in the window that was already in front (Accessibility showed that window's title become the tab's title), so you land on the right tab either way. The Accessibility raise did not run there: when two windows show the same title the picker refuses to choose, on purpose. A first Dia run was unreliable because both windows showed "New Tab"; the rerun gave the second window a title of its own. **Two windows on different spaces or profiles** (the front one put on another space or profile, then `Place.go` to the tab in the first): **Dia** went `exact` and the first window came to the front on your profile, twice in a row; **Arc** went `exact` and the front window ended on your space (Arc moved that window back to it; the first window was not raised by id, but you are where you were). Dia's Accessibility window title is "profile name: page title" (checked on the real app), which the picker now strips before comparing (`WindowRaiser.pick`, three new tests), so a Dia tab with a short title can be matched in a multi-window Dia; before, only titles of 6 or more characters could be, by a partial match. Closing a window in Arc is not safe to script: `close front window` with two windows open closed both (a new window brought back the 3 tabs and 6 spaces unharmed), and closing by id through a loop reference did nothing. **Who raises the window in Dia:** the same set-up (the first window on your profile, a second on another) with only the tab-switching script, no Accessibility raise, answered `ok` and put the front window back on your profile, but the first window was not brought forward (checked at 1 s and 3 s). `Place.go` in the same set-up did bring it forward, so the raise is the app's Accessibility step (with the profile stripped from the title), not Dia. **Three windows:** Arc, with two extra windows on other spaces: `Place.go` answered `exact` and the front window ended on your space (the first window was not raised). Dia, with two extra windows on other profiles (set up on the third try; the first two attempts failed to keep the profile): `Place.go` answered `exact` and the front window ended on your profile, but the first window was not brought forward, unlike the two-window run, probably because two windows then show the same title and the picker refuses to choose between them. **Arc left with extra windows, and how they were cleaned up:** after the three-window run Arc's scripting list held 3 windows and two of them could not be read; `close` by index, by id and by `whose` all answered `ok` and changed nothing. A look at the window list (CoreGraphics: owner, on-screen flag and size only, no titles) showed two on-screen Arc windows, and pressing the close button of the one behind through Accessibility closed it: one on-screen window is left, with its 3 tabs and 6 spaces, while the scripting list still holds two stale entries. Do not make windows in Arc from a script. **Still never run on the real browsers:** more than three windows, and windows in other macOS desktops (Spaces). An agent cannot grant Automation. `tools/check_space_browsers.sh` is a read-only check you can run yourself: it prints the space or profile of each running browser's front window, no address or title.

Not verified (an agent cannot grant Automation, show a real screen or change your volume): browsers' tab switching, Finder, Terminal, Firefox and Electron, and every item above on your own windows. What is tested: the rules for what may be opened, the working-set choice, the scroll and cursor arithmetic, the order of raising, the flags passed to the app, the retries, the link parsing and what may be put away. The URL event handler and Dock menu (`FocusAppDelegate`) are compile-checked only. A Firefox, Terminal or Electron tab bar may expose its tabs differently from what `WindowRaiser.tabButtons` expects.

## Not built: ranked suggestions for you to pick from
1. ~~Quiet only the window you drifted into.~~ Built (see above); still needs a look on a real display: overlapping and moving windows decide whether it feels right.
2. ~~Pick up where you left off (a chip with the saved next step).~~ Gone: the Next step feature was removed on 2026-10-17 (owner: bloat). "Back to your spot" below does the same job with no typing.
3. ~~Learn what works for you.~~ Built as a measurement only (see "What brings you back"); the starting cue is not chosen from it. Original idea: Time to return is already recorded per drift. Record which cue was showing, show the medians in Insights ("The gray brings you back about 40 s sooner than the banner"), and start with the cue that works for that person. Measure before it decides anything.
4. ~~Cursor halo.~~ Built (see above). Original idea: A ring around the pointer that grows the longer you are away. Hard to ignore without covering anything. Costs a little CPU while away, so it needs a measurement against the 2 percent budget.
5. ~~Lower the sound.~~ Built (see above). Original idea: System volume eases down while you are off task on a video or music site and comes back when you return. Strong for video rabbit holes. Risks: a call or headphones in use (the call detector would have to hold it), and a crash must never leave the volume low.
6. ~~Fog.~~ Built (see above). Original idea: A progressive blur of the screen. Same private layer as the gray. Harsh, so it is your call.
7. **A macOS Focus mode at session start** (Do Not Disturb filter through Shortcuts). Removes the notifications that cause drifts. Needs the user to install a Shortcut.
8. **Drift reason after you return** (PLAN F2), left unbuilt on purpose: the owner cut the "next step" prompt on 2026-10-17 as bloat, and this is the same kind of ask after a return. One tap, "stuck, bored, tired, a notification". "Stuck" opens the next-step helper that already exists.

Not recommended: making the screen harder to use (slower scrolling, shrinking other apps' windows). It punishes a wrong call as much as a right one.

## Left out on purpose
Remembering places across sessions. A window title or page address saved to disk would break "sessions save counts, never text". The places live in memory and go with the session.

## Not verified
Raising a real window with Accessibility, switching real Safari or Chrome tabs, and how the ring and the dimmed edges look on a real display were not run: an agent cannot grant Accessibility, Automation or look at the screen. What was checked: the choice of place, the window matching, the AppleScript compiles in Safari's and Chrome's dictionaries (`osacompile`), the controller's behavior, the ring's timing and position math, and the menu rendered offscreen. A two-minute check for you: open two TextEdit documents and a Safari window with two tabs, start a session on a task that fits the first, drift to the second tab, then another app, and press Back to task. It should land on the first document, then the first tab.
